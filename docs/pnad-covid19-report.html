Brazil’s PNAD COVID-19 household survey¶

FIAP postgraduate program in Data Analytics · Tech Challenge 3

What did households report about testing, symptoms, distancing and earnings between August and October 2020? This analysis uses interviews from the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics (IBGE). Counts describe survey records. The same person could be interviewed in multiple months.

1. Data architecture¶

The project stores the survey files in Google Cloud Storage. Spark on Dataproc writes the bronze and silver datasets and loads the analytical tables into BigQuery. The local snapshot contains 2,650,459 interview records from May through November 2020. The analysis below uses three of those months.

111,496 interviews with a positive or negative test result
28,109 interviews with at least one positive result

2. Questionnaire and study population¶

The study follows 19 substantive questions. A positive result on any of the three tests classifies an interview as positive. Otherwise, at least one negative result is required for the comparison group. Records reporting only inconclusive, pending or missing results are excluded.

Positive results by month and sex¶

The number of interviews with a reported positive result rose from 7,639 in August to 10,951 in October. The counts below are unweighted. Differences in the number or composition of interviews and in access to tests can affect the comparison.

Age and reported symptoms¶

The original age intervals use an open lower endpoint, so age zero is excluded from the age charts. The symptom grouping follows the project’s descriptive rule. Difficulty breathing takes precedence, followed by fever, followed by all other responses. These groups describe reported symptoms and have no clinical validation.

Distancing and healthcare use¶

Each distancing panel uses the interviews with an identified distancing response in that test-result group. For the healthcare chart, the project compares an explicit “Yes” with all other answers. Missing or inapplicable answers are included in the latter category.

Earnings and education¶

The cash-earnings chart uses positive test interviews with reported earnings above zero and at most BRL 70,000. The education panels show counts and shares within each test-result group.

3. Implications for service planning¶

The original project proposed using the differences by age, earnings and geography to guide questions about testing access, outreach and demand for care. The survey can identify which groups reported symptoms or care-seeking most often in this sample. Estimating population prevalence requires survey weights and design information. Prioritizing individual patients would require clinical evidence beyond these questionnaire responses.
